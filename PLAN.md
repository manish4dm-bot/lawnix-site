## 2. The arithmetic

$1M a year at a $60 average order is about 16,700 orders, or 45 a day averaged across
the year. Lawn tools are seasonal: roughly 70% of US demand lands March to August. So
the real target is about 80 orders a day in season and 15 a day off season.

One SKU does not get there. Rocklin, the category leader, built its business on one
rake plus accessories; the ceiling for a single leveling rake in the US is in the low
millions a year and it is already contested. Lawnix reaches $1M by stacking:

| Lever | Share of $1M | How |
|---|---|---|
| Leveling rakes on Amazon (US) | 45% | LX-30 and LX-48 ranking page 1 for "lawn leveling rake" |
| Lawn-prep line extensions on Amazon | 25% | 4 to 6 more SKUs that the same buyer needs (see section 5) |
| lawnixindustry.com direct | 15% | SEO guides, email list, returning customers, bundles |
| Wholesale and landscapers | 10% | Case packs to local landscapers, garden centers, Faire |
| Amazon Canada / UK | 5% | Same listings, FBA export or local FBA |

## 3. Unit economics (replace assumptions first)

LX-30 at $54.99, FBA, *assumptions* in italics:

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Sale price | $54.99 |
| Amazon referral fee 15% | -$8.25 |
| FBA fulfilment fee (large standard, ~6 lb, long item) | *-$12 to -$16* |
| Landed cost (factory + freight + duty) | *-$14 to -$18* |
| Packaging and prep | *-$1.50* |
| Returns and defects allowance 3% | -$1.65 |
| **Contribution before ads** | **about $12 to $17** |
| Ads at 25% TACoS during launch | -$13.75 |
| **Profit per unit during launch** | **about break-even** |
| Ads at 10% TACoS once ranked | -$5.50 |
| **Profit per unit at steady state** | **about $7 to $11** |

Reading: at $54.99 the LX-30 only works once it ranks. Two protections:
1. Check whether the LX-30 ships as "large standard" or tips into oversize. A 30-inch
   plate on a two-piece handle in a compact box is the difference between a $13 and a
   $25 fulfilment fee. Design the box around Amazon's size tiers before the first order.
2. The LX-48 ($74.99) and bundles carry more margin per order. Push mix toward them.

Direct orders on the website keep the 15% referral fee but pay Stripe 2.9% + 30¢ and
MCF fulfilment (higher than FBA). Net effect: website orders are worth about $5 more
each than Amazon orders, and you own the customer.

## 4. Phases

### Phase 0: Launch readiness (now to first inventory)
- Publish the site. Collect launch-list emails from day one.
- Confirm supplier, sample, and test: plate flatness, weld quality, handle thread, box
  size vs Amazon size tier, drop test. Fix before ordering.
- Register the brand: USPTO application for LAWNIX (word mark, class 8 hand tools).
  Amazon Brand Registry accepts a pending application. Without it there is no A+ content,
  no Brand Store, no Sponsored Brands, no Brand Referral Bonus.
- Decide warranty (12 months is safe; lifetime is a strong differentiator if the steel holds).
- Decide shipping policy for direct orders (free over $50 is the usual answer).
- Product photography: real photos the week samples land. Replace the generated images.
- First order: *assumption* 500 LX-30, 200 LX-48, 300 RC-01. Enough to not stock out in
  the first 60 days of ranking, small enough to survive a mistake.

### Phase 1: First 90 days on Amazon (target $20K to $40K)
- Listings: title built from the top search terms ("lawn leveling rake", "lawn leveler",
  "level lawn rake 30 inch", "topdressing rake"), seven images including size comparison
  and in-use, A+ content, video.
- Reviews: Amazon Vine (30 units per parent) on day one. Insert card with QR to the
  leveling guide, not to a review ask.
- Ads: three Sponsored Products campaigns per SKU (exact, broad, auto), daily budget
  *$60 to $100* total, target 25 to 30% TACoS, cut to 15% by day 60. Use the PPC Spider
  God Mode playbook for bid rules.
- Price: launch at $49.99 with a coupon, step to $54.99 once 50 reviews are in.
- Off-Amazon: send launch-list emails with Amazon Attribution links so the Brand
  Referral Bonus returns about 10% of those sales.

### Phase 2: Season one (March to August next year, target $250K to $400K)
- Rank page 1 for the core terms before March. Season demand does the rest.
- Add 3 line extensions ready to sell by February (section 5).
- Launch bundles on the website: rake + cultivator, rake + spreader scoop. Bundles lift
  average order from $55 to $90 and make the direct channel worth the effort.
- SEO: one guide a week on lawnixindustry.com (leveling, topdressing, sand vs compost,
  fixing dog spots, overseeding after leveling). These are the searches that lead to the
  purchase. Target 10,000 organic visits a month by August.
- Wholesale: a one-page line sheet, case-pack pricing, Faire listing, outreach to 50
  landscapers in each of 5 metro areas with lawn-culture (Dallas, Atlanta, Phoenix,
  Charlotte, Tampa).
- Content: short vertical videos of before and after leveling. This category performs
  on TikTok and YouTube Shorts; the lawn-care creator scene is large and reachable.

### Phase 3: Year two (target $1M run rate)
- 8 to 10 SKUs, 2 bundles, Amazon US plus Canada.
- Website doing 15% of revenue with 30% of orders from returning customers and email.
- Wholesale at 10% with 25+ active accounts.
- Ads under 10% TACoS on hero SKUs; new SKUs launched into an existing brand audience.

## 5. Product roadmap (the lawn-prep system)

Same buyer, same season, same search session. Each adds basket size and ranking strength.

1. **Topdressing spreader scoop** (hand scoop for sand) - cheap, high attach rate.
2. **Lawn aerator sandals or manual core aerator** - "aerate before you level".
3. **Hand edger / half-moon edger** in stainless - matches the brand promise.
4. **Thatch rake** - spring prep, same buyer.
5. **Replacement grip and handle kit** - keeps the "lifetime tool" story honest.
6. **LX-36** - fills the gap between 30 and 48; the size most competitors sell.
7. **Push broom for working sand in** - the step after raking.
8. Later: battery tools are a different business; stay in hand tools until $1M.

Rule for every new SKU: the same person buys it the same month, it fits an FBA
standard-size tier, and it carries at least $12 contribution before ads.

## 6. Channels in order of priority

1. **Amazon US.** 70% of revenue in years one and two. Everything else feeds it.
2. **lawnixindustry.com.** Guides for SEO, email capture, bundles, and the place
   wholesale and press go to check the brand is real. Stripe checkout now, Shopify when
   direct orders pass about 10 a day.
3. **Email.** Launch list from day one. Monthly during off-season, weekly in season.
4. **Short video.** Before/after leveling clips, no talking head required.
5. **Wholesale.** Faire plus direct landscaper outreach.
6. **Amazon Canada, then UK/EU.** Only after the US is profitable.

## 9. Risks that end this plan early

- **Oversize fulfilment fees.** Kills the LX-30 margin. Settle the box before ordering.
- **Weld or plate failure in the field.** One viral "it snapped" video on a $55 tool is
  hard to recover from. Over-build the first batch.
- **Stockout in May.** Order the season's stock by January; sea freight is 6 to 8 weeks.
- **No Brand Registry.** File the trademark now.
- **Competing on price with the incumbents.** Compete on stainless, width, handle and
  guide content instead. The $5 cheaper rake always exists.
