How to level a lawn with a leveling rake

A bumpy lawn is fixed with sand, patience and the right rake. Here is the method homeowners use to get a flat, even surface in one season.

When to level

Level when the grass is actively growing so it can push through the sand within a couple of weeks. For cool-season grass that is spring or early autumn. For warm-season grass, late spring through summer.

What you need

A leveling rake, dry masonry sand (or a sand and compost blend for thin lawns), a wheelbarrow and a hose. Budget about one cubic yard of sand per 1,000 square feet for a light application.

Step 1: Mow low and bag

Cut the lawn one or two settings lower than usual and collect the clippings. Shorter grass lets the sand reach the soil instead of sitting on the blades.

Step 2: Spread the sand

Tip piles of sand across the low areas. Do not try to cover the whole lawn at once; work in sections you can finish in an hour.

Step 3: Rake it level

Pull the leveling rake toward you in long, overlapping passes, then push it back. The plate drops sand into dips and skims across the high spots. Keep the handle low and let the plate float.

Step 4: Brush and water

Once the grass tips show through, water the area well so the sand settles. Keep off it for a few days.

Step 5: Repeat for deep dips

Never bury grass under more than about half an inch of sand at a time. Deep hollows take two or three rounds, a few weeks apart.
